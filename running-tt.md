# Analyze 4 × 2km @ 4:10/km / 120s Recovery — Threshold Tolerance

I will upload a Garmin TCX file from a running workout:

**4 × 2km @ approximately 4:10/km**
**120 seconds recovery between reps**

Analyze the workout using the actual TCX data only.

## Primary objective

The purpose of this workout is **threshold tolerance**, not VO₂max, maximum speed, or a final-rep test.

The key question is:

> Can I maintain approximately 4:10/km near threshold for progressively longer periods while keeping HR drift and fatigue under control?

Do NOT reward a faster final rep automatically. A controlled final rep is preferable to a faster but excessively high-HR final rep.

# 3. Analyze the KEY CONCEPTS

Analyze these concepts separately:

### A. Threshold Pace

Determine whether approximately 4:10/km appears to be a sustainable threshold-oriented pace based on:

* HR behavior
* pace consistency
* within-rep HR rise
* ability to complete all four reps
* subjective intensity only if provided

Do NOT claim an exact lactate threshold from HR alone.

---

### B. Intra-rep HR Drift — PRIMARY METRIC

For each 2km interval calculate:

**HR Drift = Finish HR − Start/early HR**

Preferably compare:

**first 1km HR vs second 1km HR**

Example:

> Rep 1: 162 → 166 = +4 bpm
> Rep 4: 165 → 171 = +6 bpm

| 3   |              |               |                 |
| 4   |              |               |                 |

Explain whether drift is:

* Stable
* Mildly increasing
* Clearly increasing
* Excessive

This is one of the most important metrics.

---

### C. Late-session HR Drift — PRIMARY METRIC

Compare equivalent sections of:

**Rep 1 vs Rep 4**

Preferably:

* first 1km HR
* second 1km HR
* finish HR

Do NOT simply compare maximum HR.

Calculate:

> Rep 4 equivalent HR − Rep 1 equivalent HR

Explain whether the higher HR is likely caused by:

1. accumulated fatigue
2. faster pace
3. both
4. measurement uncertainty

If pace differs materially between reps, explicitly adjust the interpretation.

---

### D. Pace-adjusted HR Drift

This is critical.

Do NOT label an increase in HR as HR drift if pace also became substantially faster.

Separate:

**External workload change**
from
**physiological drift**

For example:

> 4:10/km → 4:10/km + HR increases = stronger evidence of drift.

But:

> 4:10/km → 4:02/km + HR increases = cannot attribute the entire HR increase to drift.

---

### E. Threshold Tolerance

Evaluate:

> How well can I remain near threshold for the entire 2km interval without excessive HR escalation?

Look at:

* 2km duration
* HR rise within each rep
* HR rise across reps
* pace consistency
* final-rep control

Give a rating:

**Poor / Developing / Good / Very Good / Excellent**

Explain why using the actual numbers.

# 6. Assess whether 4:10/km was the correct pace

Classify it as:

### Too fast

If HR rises excessively within reps and the final rep becomes uncontrolled.

### Appropriate

If HR gradually rises but remains controlled and pace is consistent.

### Too slow

If HR remains unusually low and the final rep could easily accelerate without significant HR escalation.

The goal is **not** to maximize HR.

The goal is to find the fastest pace that allows controlled threshold tolerance.

---

## Important analysis rules

1. **Use actual TCX data first.**
2. Do not invent HR values.
3. Distinguish average HR, max HR, finish HR, and recovery HR.
4. Do not treat every HR increase as HR drift.
5. Always account for pace changes.
6. Do not infer exact lactate threshold from HR alone.
7. Do not overinterpret Garmin HR measurement noise.
8. Prioritize **intra-rep drift** and **pace-adjusted late-session drift**.
9. The goal is **threshold tolerance**, not maximum HR.
10. Separate:

* Observation
* Calculation
* Interpretation
* Training implication

Keep the analysis evidence-driven and concise, with tables as the primary presentation format.
